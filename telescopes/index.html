How these pictures are made

9 telescopes, 4 cameras, 7 filters and 4,764 hours of light. What each piece does, and what it made.

The path of light

  1. Telescope

    Gathers the light. Its focal length decides how much sky fits in the frame: short for big nebulae, long for small galaxies.

    Here: 250 to 1,764 mm, fields from 5.4° down to 37′ wide.

  2. Filter

    Lets one colour through at a time. Red, green and blue make natural colour; Hα, OIII and SII pass only the light of glowing gas.

    Here: 7 filters, L, R, G, B, Hα, OIII, SII.

  3. Camera

    An astronomy camera, its sensor cooled against noise. A mono sensor shoots each filter in turn with every pixel; a colour one takes all three at once.

    Here: 4 cameras, 3 of them mono.

  4. Mount and guiding

    Turns with the sky so the stars stay points through exposures of minutes; a small guide camera corrects it every few seconds.

  5. Software

    Runs the night, keeps the guiding, records every sub and turns hundreds of them into one picture.

    Here: Sequence Generator Pro, PHD2, AstroTracker and PixInsight.

The cameras

Each sensor drawn to scale against the largest

A mono camera has no colour of its own: every pixel records whatever the filter in front of it lets through, so a colour picture is shot one filter at a time. A colour camera has a mosaic of tiny red, green and blue filters on its pixels and takes all three at once.

Words on the picture pages

Integration
The total exposure in a picture: every sub added together. More of it means smoother faint detail.
Sub
One exposure, usually 1 to 10 minutes. Hundreds are stacked into one picture.
Kept
Subs that passed inspection. Clouded, trailed or blurred ones are left out and add no time.
Night
One night under the sky, dated by its evening.
Field
How much sky the frame covers, in degrees (°) or arcminutes (′). The full Moon is about 31′ across.
Pixel scale
How much sky one pixel sees, in arcseconds (″). Smaller shows finer detail, when the air is steady enough.
Gain
The camera's amplification, set for each camera and filter.
Sensor temperature
The sensor is cooled, often to −10 °C, to keep down the noise heat makes.
